# Sales suite

How the sales features work: what they store, which permissions they need, and their limits.

## Lead scoring

A 0–100 score on every contact: **fit** (who they are — rules on the contact's own fields) plus **engagement** (what they did — rules on events counted from the database), clamped to 0–100. Rules and the math: `packages/shared/src/scoring.ts`; API: `apps/api/src/scoring`; settings page: Settings → Data → Lead scoring (`/settings/lead-scoring`); the contact page shows `ContactScorePanel`.

- **Settings** live in `Workspace.settings.leadScoring = { enabled, hot, warm }` (defaults off, 70, 40). While **off**, nothing writes `Contact.score` — people and the lead API own it. While **on**, the computed score owns it (a hand-set score is replaced at the next recompute). `scoreFit`, `scoreEngagement`, `scoreBreakdown` (`[{ ruleId, name, points }]`) and `scoreUpdatedAt` (last change) are stored next to it and returned by `GET /contacts/:id`.
- **Rules** (`ScoringRule`): fit rules test a field (`jobTitle`, `company`, `email`, `phone`, `leadSource`, `country`, `city`, `website`, `linkedinUrl`, `lifecycleStage`, `status`, `qualification`, `priority`, `tags`, `custom.<key>`) with `is`, `is_not`, `contains` (comma-separated values: any matches, case-insensitive), `exists`, `gt`, `lt`. Engagement rules count an event in the last `decayDays` (empty: all time), `points` each, capped at `maxPoints`. Points may be negative. Events and where they are counted from:

  | Event | Counted from |
  | --- | --- |
  | `email.opened` / `email.clicked` | outbound `Message.openedAt` / `clickedAt` in the contact's conversations |
  | `email.replied` | inbound, non-auto-reply `Message` rows (email, chat, SMS) |
  | `meeting.booked` | `Meeting` rows created, not canceled |
  | `call.outcome:<outcome>` | `Call` rows with that outcome |
  | `quote.viewed` / `quote.accepted` | `Quote.firstViewedAt` / `acceptedAt` |
  | `marketing.opened` / `marketing.clicked` | `MarketingSend.openedAt` / `clickedAt` |
  | `lead.returning` | `lead.imported` DomainEvents with `returning: true` |
  | `contact.unsubscribed` / `contact.bounced` | `Contact.unsubscribedAt` / `bouncedAt` (counted once) |

  Everything is computed from database state, so recomputing is idempotent. "Use recommended rules" adds the recommended set (`RECOMMENDED_SCORING_RULES`: decision-maker title +15, phone +5, company +5, referral +10; opens +3 max 9 / 30 d, clicks +5 max 15 / 30 d, replies +20 / 60 d, meetings +25 / 90 d, call connected / callback +10, call meeting booked +25, quote viewed +10, accepted +30, marketing click +5 max 15, returning lead +10 / 30 d; unsubscribed −30, bounced −20, not interested −15, do not call −50) — only the missing ones by name, or replacing every rule.
- **Keeping scores fresh** (`scoring` queue, `scoring.processors.ts`): a **sweep** every minute finds contacts with new `Activity`, `DomainEvent`, `MessageEvent` or `Message` rows (by uuid v7 id, since a Redis cursor, with 2 minutes of overlap) or changed `Call` / `Meeting` / `Quote` rows, in workspaces that score, and queues a debounced `contact` job each (`jobId score-<contactId>`, 10 s). A **nightly** pass (02:30 UTC) recomputes contacts with engagement points so old events age out. Turning scoring on, changing rules, and **Recalculate all** (once per workspace per 10 minutes) queue a whole-workspace pass. Writes skip `Contact.updatedAt`.
- **Hot leads**: the first time a contact reaches the hot threshold, its timeline gets "Became a hot lead (score N)" (type `contact.score_hot`, actor "Lead scoring") and its owner a notification (one per owner per pass). Nothing is emitted to workflows.
- **API** (`/workspaces/:id/scoring`): `GET /` (settings, rules, hot/warm/scored counts; `contacts.read`), `PUT /settings`, `POST /rules`, `PATCH /rules/:id`, `DELETE /rules/:id`, `POST /rules/reorder`, `POST /recommended { replace }`, `POST /recalculate` (all `settings.manage`), `GET /contacts/:contactId` (`contacts.read`, ownership-scoped).

## Files

Files attached to contacts, companies, deals and quotes (`Attachment`, bytes in Postgres). Rules: `packages/shared/src/attachments.ts`; API: `apps/api/src/attachments`; UI: `AttachmentsPanel({ entityType, entityId })` (mounted on contact and company pages).

- **Limits**: 10 MB per file — uploads are base64 JSON and the API's JSON body limit is 20 MB, so 10 MB (≈13.4 MB of base64) is the most that fits with headroom; 1 GB per workspace (live files; deleted ones stop counting). Clear 400 / 409 messages for each.
- **Types**: the stored type is sniffed from the bytes (PDF, PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP, HEIC, MP4/MOV, MP3/WAV/OGG, WebM, Office/OpenDocument zips by extension, legacy Office, zip, gzip, 7z, rar, RTF), else a short list of plain-text extensions (txt, csv, tsv, md, json, ics, vcf, log, eml), else `application/octet-stream` — markup (HTML, SVG, XML) never gets a type a browser renders. Programs and scripts are refused by extension (exe, dll, msi, bat, cmd, ps1, vbs, js, jar, sh, app, apk, dmg…) and by bytes (PE `MZ`, ELF, Mach-O, `#!`). Names are cleaned (no paths, control characters or quotes).
- **Download**: always `Content-Disposition: attachment` with the stored type, `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; sandbox` and `Cache-Control: private, no-store`; signed-in only. The web app fetches through the `/api` proxy and saves the blob.
- **Permissions**: viewing needs the record's read permission (`contacts.read` for contacts and companies, `deals.read`, `quotes.read`) and being able to see the record (ownership/team visibility for contacts, deals and quotes); upload, rename and delete need its write permission (`contacts.write`, `deals.write`, `quotes.write`). Lists never select the bytes.
- **Timeline**: "File added: x.pdf" / "File removed: x.pdf" (`file.added` / `file.removed`) on the record — a quote's go on its deal, else its contact, else its company, with `quoteId` in the data. Uploads, renames and deletions are audited.
- **Delete** is soft (`deletedAt`): the row and bytes stay for the audit trail but leave lists, downloads and the workspace total. Nothing purges them yet.
- **API** (`/workspaces/:id/attachments`): `GET ?entityType&entityId` (items + `limits { fileBytes, workspaceBytes, usedBytes }`), `POST { entityType, entityId, fileName, contentType?, data }`, `GET /:id/download`, `PATCH /:id { fileName }`, `DELETE /:id`.

## Calendar and booking pages

Calendly-style scheduling: teammates connect their own Google or Microsoft calendar; the workspace publishes booking pages at `/book/<slug>`; a prospect picks a free slot and Growthdesk creates the contact, the meeting and the calendar event. Rules and slot math: `packages/shared/src/booking.ts`; API: `apps/api/src/booking`; UI: Settings → You → Calendar (`/settings/calendar`), Meetings → Booking pages (`/meetings/booking-pages`), the Meetings page's calendar control, and the public pages `apps/web/src/app/book/[slug]` and `apps/web/src/app/book/manage/[token]` (outside the `(public)` group, whose layout is Growthdesk's own marketing shell).

- **Admin setup**: calendar sign-in reuses the installation's Google and Microsoft OAuth clients (admin console → Settings → Google and Microsoft sign-in, or `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`/`MICROSOFT_CLIENT_ID` …). Each OAuth app must also list the redirect URI `${PUBLIC_APP_URL}/api/calendar/oauth/google/callback` (Google) and `${PUBLIC_APP_URL}/api/calendar/oauth/microsoft/callback` (Microsoft), and allow the calendar scopes: Google `openid email …/auth/calendar.events …/auth/calendar.readonly` (offline, consent prompt; on a Google app in "testing" or unverified, these are sensitive scopes), Microsoft `offline_access openid email User.Read Calendars.ReadWrite`. Without a client the settings page and the Meetings menu say so and show the redirect URIs; nothing fails half-way.
- **Connections** (`CalendarConnection`, personal): any member connects their own; nobody can read or change someone else's. The OAuth state is HMAC-signed (`calendar-oauth`), names the workspace, person, provider and return page (`?return=meetings` comes back to Meetings), lives 10 minutes, and the callback also requires the browser's session to be that person. Tokens are sealed (`secretSealed`, `accessSealed`), refreshed within a minute of expiry. The person picks the calendar meetings are written to and the calendars checked for conflicts (`busyCalendarIds`, empty = the write calendar). Several connections are allowed: busy times come from all; events go to the oldest. A refused refresh marks the connection `error` and notifies its owner (bell + email); it is then skipped until reconnected. An hourly `health` job proves idle connections still work.
- **Booking pages** (`BookingPage`, `booking.manage`): slug unique across the installation (a deleted page frees it), hosts must be members, weekly hours in the page's zone, duration, start-time step, buffers before/after, minimum notice, days ahead, location (`google_meet` / `teams` — the video link is created by the host's calendar provider when it matches — `phone`, `in_person`, `custom`), questions (`text`, `textarea`, `phone` — saved as the contact's phone — and at most one `consent` checkbox), confirmation message, on/off. `single` uses the first host; `round_robin` goes through the hosts in order from `roundRobinCursor`, skipping anyone busy.
- **Slots** (`computeSlots`): each weekly window, in the page's zone (DST-safe through `zonedTime`), is cut into steps from its opening; a slot needs the whole meeting inside the window and the meeting plus buffers clear of every busy interval — the host's calendar free/busy (Google `freeBusy`, Graph `calendarView`) and their scheduled Growthdesk meetings — and must fall between now + notice and now + days ahead. Slots are returned grouped by the visitor's own days (their zone is detected and can be changed). A host whose calendar can't be read right now offers no slots for that request (no double bookings); one whose access was revoked is skipped.
- **Booking** (`POST /public/booking/:slug/book`): re-checks the slot against the calendar, then inside a transaction takes a per-host advisory lock and re-checks Growthdesk's meetings, so two visitors racing for one slot get one meeting and one 409. The contact goes through `ContactsService.upsert` (source `booking`, matched by email then phone, blanks filled, owner = host if it had none); a full plan or an erased person still gets the meeting, without a contact. A ticked consent box records `granted` consent through `ConsentService` (source "Booking page: <title>", IP and user agent) — what AI calling requires. The `Meeting` has `source: "booking"`, attendee name/email/zone, answers, a random `manageToken`; `meeting.booked` is logged on the timeline and emitted (same payload as a recorded meeting: `{ contactId, dealId, startsAt }`), so workflows and the engine's stop-on-meeting see it. Then the event is created on the host's calendar with the attendee invited (`sendUpdates=all` / Graph's own invitation) and a Meet / Teams link when chosen; without a connected calendar the booking still stands and the host's notification says it was not added. The host is notified (bell + email).
- **Attendee email** (platform mail, `booking_booked` / `booking_rescheduled` / `booking_canceled` / `booking_reminder`): time in their zone, video link, an "Add to calendar" `.ics` link (`GET /public/booking/manage/:token/ics` — platform mail has no attachments) and the private manage link `/book/manage/<token>`. A reminder goes out 24 h before (delayed `reminder` job on the `calendar` queue; it re-checks the meeting is still on at that time).
- **Cancel / reschedule** by token: cancel deletes the calendar event (attendees told), logs `meeting.canceled`, notifies the host; reschedule keeps the same host, re-checks the new slot (the meeting's own old interval does not block it), moves the event, logs `meeting.rescheduled`. In the app, cancelling or moving a booked meeting (`PATCH /meetings/:id`) also cancels or moves its calendar event (`booking/meeting-sync.ts`; a failure is retried by a `meeting.sync` job). `GET /meetings` returns `source`, `attendeeName`/`attendeeEmail`, `conferenceUrl`, `answers`, `bookingPage { id, title, slug }` and never the manage token.
- **Rate limits** (per IP): public reads 60/min, bookings and changes 10/min (`BOOKING_RATE_LIMIT` sets the writes; reads are six times that).
- **API**: `/workspaces/:id/calendar` — `GET connections`, `GET oauth/:provider/start?return=settings|meetings`, `GET connections/:id/calendars`, `PUT connections/:id { calendarId, busyCalendarIds }`, `DELETE connections/:id` (members, own only); `GET /calendar/oauth/:provider/callback` (public, redirect). `/workspaces/:id/booking-pages` — `GET`, `POST`, `GET /:id`, `PATCH /:id`, `DELETE /:id` (`booking.manage`). Public `/public/booking`: `GET /:slug`, `GET /:slug/slots?from&to&timezone` (≤ 42 days per call), `POST /:slug/book`, `GET /manage/:token`, `POST /manage/:token/cancel { reason }`, `POST /manage/:token/reschedule { start }`, `GET /manage/:token/ics`.
- **Test fakes**: `GOOGLE_OAUTH_URL`, `GOOGLE_USERINFO_URL`, `GOOGLE_CALENDAR_API_URL`, `MICROSOFT_LOGIN_URL`, `MICROSOFT_GRAPH_API_URL` override the providers' base URLs (tests only; never set in production). `apps/api/test/booking.e2e-spec.ts` runs against a local fake of Google.
- **Not done yet**: no two-way sync (events created or moved directly in Google/Outlook don't update the Meeting; the calendar's busy time still blocks slots); round robin is "next free in order", not load-balanced; a reschedule offers the page's slots for any host and refuses one the original host isn't free for; Microsoft is covered by the adapter but only Google is exercised end-to-end in tests; no per-page branding beyond the workspace name.

## Products and quotes

A product catalogue, quotes (proposals) built from it, and a public page where the buyer views, accepts and signs. Rules and money math: `packages/shared/src/quotes.ts` (tests `packages/shared/test/quotes.test.ts`); API: `apps/api/src/quotes`; UI: Sales → Products (`/products`), Sales → Quotes (`/quotes`, editor `/quotes/<id>`), `DealQuotesPanel({ dealId })` for the deal page, and the buyer's page `apps/web/src/app/q/[token]` (outside the `(public)` group, like `/book`: that group's frame is Growthdesk's own marketing shell, out of place on a seller's document).

- **Permissions**: `quotes.read` lists products and views quotes; `quotes.write` creates, edits, sends, copies the link of, duplicates, voids and deletes drafts; `products.manage` edits the catalogue and is the only role that sees `costPrice` (and so margin) — the API returns `costPrice: null` to everyone else. Quotes follow deal visibility: without `records.view_all` a person sees quotes they own and unowned ones; a deal's panel shows every quote on a deal the person can see.
- **Products** (`Product`): name, SKU, description, price, currency, billing period (`one_time` / `monthly` / `quarterly` / `yearly`), tax %, plus category, group (tiers of one offering share it, e.g. "Guest Posts"), unit and cost. Archive hides a product from the editor's picker; delete is allowed because a quote line keeps its own copy of name, price, tax and period (`productId` is just a reference, set null on delete). **Synced products** (`source ≠ ""`, from the client portal's catalog sync, `apps/api/src/catalog-sync`) are read-only here: editing any synced column (name, SKU, description, category, group, unit, price, cost, currency, billing period, tax) or archiving/deleting answers 409 "This product is synced from the client portal; change it there." On quote lines they work like any product. The products page groups rows by group, then category, and shows cost and margin columns to `products.manage` when any product has a cost.
- **Money**: every amount is decimal text parsed into integer units (cents; hundredths for quantities and percentages) and multiplied in BigInt — never a float. Per line: gross = qty × price, discount = gross × discount%, net = gross − discount (stored as `QuoteLineItem.total`), tax = net × tax%; each step rounds half away from zero to the cent. Quote: subtotal = Σ gross, discountTotal, taxTotal, total = Σ (net + tax). Recurring lines are summed per period, tax included, and described as "$1,200 one-time + $300/month" (`describeQuoteTotals`); `total` counts each recurring line once. Inputs over what `Decimal(14,2)` holds are refused. Negative prices (credit lines) are allowed; quantities are 0.01 – 100,000,000, percentages 0–100.
- **Numbers**: `Q-0001` per workspace (`number` = highest + 1). Two creations at once can pick the same number; the unique `(workspaceId, number)` refuses the second and it retries with the next one (up to 8 times). Numbers of deleted drafts are not reused.
- **Lifecycle**: `draft → sent → viewed → accepted | declined`, plus `void` (withdrawn) and `expired`. **Expiry is computed, not stored**: a sent or viewed quote whose `validUntil` (a date, read in UTC; valid through the end of that day) has passed reads as `expired` everywhere — lists (`?status=expired` matches it, `?status=sent` doesn't), the editor, the public page — and cannot be accepted; nothing needs a sweep (the `QuotesProcessorsModule` is empty on purpose). Editing a sent, viewed, declined or expired quote turns it back into a **draft** (clearing the decline) — the buyer's page then says it is being updated and offers no accept — until it is sent or its link copied again. Accepted and void quotes are **frozen** (edits 409; duplicate to make a new version). Only drafts can be deleted (soft, `deletedAt`); void anything not accepted.
- **Creating from a deal** (`POST /quotes { dealId }`, or "New quote" on the deal panel) fills whatever is left out from the deal: contact, company (else the contact's company), owner (else the creator), currency and title (the deal's name); valid until defaults to 30 days out.
- **Sending** (`POST /quotes/:id/send { mailboxId?, to?, subject?, message? }`): the email goes through `OutboundEmailService` exactly like an inbox compose — suppression and opt-out checks, mailbox daily counter, plan allowance, write-ahead `Message` — from the quote owner's own connected mailbox by default (the one they connected), else the connected mailbox with most room today. It is filed in the contact's conversations (only when `to` is the contact's address) with the conversation linked to the deal; sending the same quote again from the same mailbox threads onto the earlier email (`In-Reply-To`, same conversation), found through the `quote.sent` activity's `messageId`. The body is the message plus "View and accept quote Q-0001 ($… ): <link>". A draft becomes `sent`; `sentAt` is the latest send. Refusals map like the inbox: 409 `send_failed` (deferred/failed/uncertain), 422 `blocked`. **Copy link** (`POST /quotes/:id/link`) returns the URL and marks a draft sent, for quotes shared another way. `GET /quotes/:id/send-options` gives the send dialog its mailboxes and defaults, so sending needs `quotes.write` only (not `integrations.read`).
- **Public page** (`/q/<token>`, API `/public/quotes/:token`, 60 reads/min and 10 writes/min per IP; writes are `@SameOrigin`, so a cross-site form cannot accept on anyone's behalf). The token is 48 random hex characters, one per quote, and the only credential; lookups after it are scoped to the quote's workspace. The first view by someone who is not a member of the quote's workspace sets `viewed`, `firstViewedAt`, logs "Quote … opened by the buyer" and notifies the owner (bell); every such view bumps `viewCount` / `lastViewedAt` (teammates previewing don't count). Drafts (being revised) and void quotes show who they are from, not their content. The page shows the workspace's legal name and postal address (Settings → Company), the owner as sender, the lines, totals, terms; "Download PDF" is the browser's print dialog with an A4 print stylesheet that keeps only the document (and the signature block once signed). It follows the visitor's light/dark theme; the printout is always light.
- **Accept** (`POST /public/quotes/:token/accept { signerName, signerEmail, signerTitle?, signature: { type: "typed" | "drawn", data }, agree: true }`): refused (409, with the reason) unless the quote is sent/viewed and not expired. The status is claimed with a conditional update, so two acceptances record one. In the same transaction: the signer's name, email, title, signature (typed text, or a PNG data URL ≤ ~200 KB from the signing canvas), IP (`clientIpFrom`, trusted proxy hops) and browser; `documentHash` = sha256 of the canonical JSON of the quote's content as stored at that moment (`quote-document.ts`: workspace, quote id, number, title, currency, valid until, intro, terms, contact/company/deal ids, every line in order with fixed-scale amounts, totals — keys sorted); the activity "Quote … accepted and signed by …"; a **high-priority task** for the owner "Quote Q-0012 accepted — close the deal", due in a day; and the `quote.accepted` event `{ quoteId, quoteNumber, dealId, contactId, total, currency }` (subject `quote`) for workflows. After commit the owner is notified (bell + email). The editor recomputes the hash from the stored rows and shows "Content unchanged since signing" (`acceptance.hashMatches`).
- **Deal rule**: on acceptance, if the quote's deal is still valued at **0**, its value becomes the quote's **one-time total** (tax included) — or, for a quote with only recurring lines, its total. A deal someone already valued is left alone. The deal's stage is not moved; closing it is the owner's task.
- **Decline** (`POST /public/quotes/:token/decline { reason }`): same open-status check; records `declinedAt` and the reason, logs it, notifies the owner (bell + email). No workflow trigger.
- **What the e-signature proves, and what it doesn't**: it is a record that someone holding the quote's link, giving this name and email, ticked "I agree", typed or drew a signature, at this time, from this IP address and browser, for exactly the content whose sha256 is stored. It does not verify identity (no email confirmation code, no ID check), uses no certificate or third-party timestamp, and the hash lives in our own database. That is generally enough for a simple electronic signature on a commercial quote (ESIGN / eIDAS "simple"), not an advanced or qualified one.
- **API** (`/workspaces/:id`): `GET products?q&state=active|archived|all`, `GET products/:id` (`quotes.read` or `products.manage`), `POST products`, `PATCH products/:id { …, archived }`, `DELETE products/:id` (`products.manage`); `GET quotes?status&dealId&contactId&ownerId=me|<id>&q&sort=number|createdAt|updatedAt|total|validUntil`, `GET quotes/:id`, `GET deals/:dealId/quotes` (`quotes.read`); `POST quotes`, `PATCH quotes/:id` (`lineItems` replaces all lines, in order), `DELETE quotes/:id`, `POST quotes/:id/duplicate | void | send | link`, `GET quotes/:id/send-options` (`quotes.write`). Amounts are fixed-scale decimal strings (`"1200.00"`). Void is audited.
- **Not done yet**: no PDF file is generated server-side (the buyer prints to PDF); no countersignature by the seller; no e-mail confirmation of the signer's address; no reminders for unopened or expiring quotes; no per-quote branding or logo (workspaces have no logo yet — the page uses the workspace's name and company profile); currency is per quote with no conversion (a product priced in another currency is copied at its number); the deal panel is not mounted on the deal page by this feature.

## Forecast, quotas and stale deals

What the team will close in a month or quarter, against targets; and deals that have stopped moving. Math and inputs: `packages/shared/src/forecast.ts` (unit tests in `packages/shared/test/forecast.test.ts`); API: `apps/api/src/forecast` (+ deal fields in `apps/api/src/pipeline`); page: Sales → Forecast (`/forecast`); e2e: `apps/api/test/forecast.e2e-spec.ts`.

- **Periods** are calendar months and quarters in the **workspace's time zone** (`?periodType=month|quarter&periodStart=YYYY-MM-DD`; any day in the period names it; default: this month). Won deals count by `closedAt` inside the period's instants; open deals by `expectedCloseDate` (a plain date) between its first and last day.
- **Deal fields**: `forecastCategory` (`pipeline` default, `best_case`, `commit`, `omitted`), `nextStep`, `nextStepAt` (a calendar date, UTC midnight) — set through `POST/PATCH /deals`, logged on the timeline when changed. Omitted deals are listed but never summed.
- **Figures** (per owner, per team, total): target, won, commit, best case, pipeline, omitted, weighted pipeline (open, not omitted: value × stage probability), projected (won + commit), upside (+ best case), gap (target − won; negative = beaten), attainment (won ÷ target), coverage (open pipeline ÷ gap), deals won and meetings booked (meetings created in the period, not canceled) against their targets.
- **Currency**: single-currency. Only deals and revenue targets in the workspace currency are summed; other currencies are listed under `otherCurrencies` and counted as deals, never converted. A revenue target in another currency is refused (and ignored if the workspace currency later changes — `ignoredTargets`).
- **Targets** (`SalesTarget`, permission `targets.manage`): `GET /targets?periodType&periodStart`, `PUT /targets` (upsert `{ userId | teamId | neither, periodType, periodStart, metric: revenue_won | deals_won | meetings_booked, target, currency? }`), `DELETE /targets/:id`. A rep's target has `userId`; a team's `teamId`; the workspace's neither (userId and teamId are mutually exclusive). There is no unique index (NULLs never collide), so the upsert takes a transaction-scoped advisory lock on the owner+period+metric key. Count targets are whole numbers. Changes are audited (`target.set`, `target.deleted`).
- **Forecast** (`GET /forecast`, `deals.read`; `?ownerId=me|<id>`, `?teamId=`): `total`, `owners[]`, `teams[]`, `deals[]`. The workspace total is measured against the workspace target, else the reps' targets added up. **Teams**: each team's roll-up is its members' rows added up (a person can be in several teams) against the team target, else its members' targets; a **deactivated** member's open pipeline is left out of team roll-ups and the team filter, their won deals stay. `?teamId=` narrows everything to the team's members and measures the total against the team's target.
- **Visibility**: with `records.view_all` or `analytics.team`, everyone's figures, the workspace target and team roll-ups. Without, `scope: "own"`: what `ownershipScope` allows elsewhere (own, unowned, team mates under team visibility) and only those people's targets.
- **Stale deals**: a stage's `rotDays` ("Stale after N days" in Settings → Pipelines; null = never). Last progress = the latest of entering the stage, a timeline entry on the deal (except the stale notice itself), a completed task, a held (completed) meeting, a completed call. An open deal is stale when now − last progress > rotDays days. Deals carry `stale: { since, days } | null` (computed per request, `apps/api/src/forecast/progress.ts`); `GET /deals?stale=true` lists only stale ones. The board shows "Stale · Nd" and has a Stale filter; the deal page shows a callout.
- **Stale sweep**: hourly at :35 UTC on the `sales` queue (`forecast.processors.ts`, `StaleDealsService.sweep`). For each newly stale deal (`staleNotifiedAt` null or older than its last progress) it claims the notice (sets `staleNotifiedAt`), writes a `deal.stale` timeline entry, emits the `deal.stale` domain event (`{ dealId, contactId, ownerId, stageId, stage, days, since }`, subject = the deal) for workflows and webhooks, and notifies the owner — once per stale spell. Any progress ends the spell; moving the deal resets it.
- **Next step nudge**: `POST /deals/:id/move` into an open stage on a deal with no next step returns the deal plus `warning` (the move still happens); the web shows it as a toast.
- **Stage guidance**: `PipelineStage.guidance` (≤ 1000 chars, one point per line), edited per stage in Settings → Pipelines; shown on the deal page under the stage and as an info tooltip in the board's column header. Left out of a pipeline save, a stage keeps its guidance (and its rotDays).
- **Note for the `sales` queue**: one BullMQ worker (`SalesProcessor`) consumes it. Another feature that needs jobs on `sales` (quote expiry) should add its job names to that processor rather than a second `@Processor(QUEUES.sales)`, which would take jobs meant for the other.
